In this project, I need to design a 'community hub' for BlaBla Corp, which is an online communication and interaction platform built around specific interest groups. Unlike general social media, the core of a community hub lies in customized design catering to the needs of a specific group, thereby providing more targeted functions and experiences. Therefore, the key to this project is not simply replicating the features of existing social platforms, but identifying the real needs of a particular community and building a meaningful functional structure accordingly.

I chose to define the target community as the 'diving community.' This choice stems not only from my personal diving experience but also from my observation of the diving activity itself. Unlike many hobbies, diving has a strong dependence on social interaction and safety. Divers usually need companions for their activities and also rely on reliable information to assess diving site environments and risks. This means that the diving community requires not only social connections but also information sharing and collaborative decision-making.

By observing the current situation of the diving community, I found that diving-related information and social interactions are often fragmented. For example, divers may look for dive buddies in Facebook groups, communicate through WhatsApp or WeChat, search for dive site information on different websites, or get advice through offline dive shops. This fragmented information structure forces users to switch between multiple platforms when planning a diving activity, which is not only inefficient but also prone to information inconsistencies.

In addition, diving activities themselves carry certain risks. For example, the current strength, visibility, temperature, and depth requirements at different dive sites all affect diving safety. At the same time, there are differences in experience, certification levels, and areas of interest among different divers. Without clear information and a matching mechanism, divers find it difficult to determine whether a particular dive is suitable for them or whether a dive buddy has sufficient experience. This lack of transparency in information further amplifies the uncertainty in diving activities.

Based on the above analysis, I have initially proposed the concept of the community platform Easy Dive. This platform aims to integrate various diving-related needs, including dive site information recording, dive buddy matching, social communication, and personal diving profile management. Users can search for or create dive sites on the platform, record the environmental characteristics of these sites (such as currents, visibility, temperature, and common marine life), and also view other users' diving experiences and interests, thereby facilitating better social interaction and collaboration.

However, while proposing this multifunctional structure, I also realized a key issue: the richness of features may lead to increased system complexity. If a platform attempts to meet too many demands at once, it may result in a cluttered interface, unclear user paths, and a significant rise in development difficulty. Therefore, during the early stages of the project, it is necessary to further analyze and prioritize these features to ensure that the system can still provide users with a clear and effective experience while remaining feasible. 

Thus, in the subsequent design process, I will focus on exploring the following questions: In the diving community, which features are core requirements and which are auxiliary? How can we maintain the completeness of features while avoiding excessive system complexity? And under the constraints of technical implementation and time limits, which features should be prioritized for implementation?
